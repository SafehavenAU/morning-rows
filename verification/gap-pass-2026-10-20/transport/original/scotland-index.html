Road accident/Casualty data

Injury road accidents reported to the police are recorded on a ‘Stats 19’ form. This data is then submitted to Transport Scotland by the police forces.

  • Stats 19 illustrative forms: show the information collected. Reported Road Casualties Scotland includes copies, and outlines what might also be collected for local use.
  • Stats 20 instructions for the completion of the returns: detailed notes of guidance on the accidents to be reported and on points on individual fields.
  • Stats 21 procedures for submitting road accident data: details of record layouts and validity checks etc. Further details are available below on the Stats 19 form.

Data quality review

It has been known for some time that there have been issues with some of the STATS 19 accident data that are collected by the police, due to the design of the form and information available to officers at the scene of an accident. Where possible these issues are rectified at each quinquennial review. The following document lists the current STATS 19 variables, issues with quality, their usage and possible solutions.

  • STATS 19 data quality issues

STATS 19 form design research project

Transport Research Laboratory were commissioned by Transport Scotland to review the currently used versions of the STATS19 form by the legacy police forces in Scotland and produce a new form that incorporates recommendations for improvement that could be potentially rolled out nationally to help improve the quality of the personal injury road accident data collected in Scotland. The report was published on 14 May 2015.

The report details the methodology used, findings from a literature review and consultation with stakeholders as well as details about the design and testing of the proposed form. All consultation participants’ comments have been anonymised to protect confidentiality.

  • Road accident data collection – form design research project

A copy of the 2015 Illustrative STATS19 form produced as part of this project is also available

Safety Camera Data

  • Road traffic accident and causality data collected by Police Scotland via the Stats 19 form is further validated by partners in each safety camera partnership and then submitted to the Scottish Safety Camera Programme Office.
  • Speed data is collected either via fixed speed loops located in the road surface or by means of mobile speed monitoring equipment.
  • Offender data is recorded on the safety camera partnership back office system. Aggregated figures are then submitted to the Scottish Safety Camera Programme Office via audited forms.

ScotStat and Transport Committees

ScotStat is a network for users and providers of Scottish Official statistics. It aims to improve communication amongst those interested in particular statistics and facilitate the setting up of working groups on specific statistical issues. There are two Transport and Travel related ScotStat Committees:

  • Transport and Travel Statistics Advisory Committee, and the
  • Liaison Group on Road Accident Statistics

Both meet about once a year with members being consulted by e-mail, between meetings, where necessary e.g. for consultation, to follow up on discussion from meetings, to notify of changes to work strands.

If you are keen to find out more about work relating to Transport and Travel Statistics or any other facet of the work of the Scottish Government Statistics Group, you can register with ScotStat to receive updates. For more information, including a feedback form and information on the work of the ScotStat Board visit the ScotStat homepage.

You may also be interested in the (UK-wide) Transport Statistics Users Group, which tends to hold one "Scottish" meeting per year.

Liaison Group on Road Accident Statistics

The group was established because there was a need for general liaison on road accident statistics for Scotland, covering both the "STAT 19" system and other statistical matters on road accidents. It meets periodically to discuss such statistical matters.

Remit

Background to the formation of the group

The notes in this section are based upon the points made in the first section of a paper entitled "Need for Liaison and Current Issues", which was prepared for the first meeting of the group, which was held on 27 June 1989. They have been updated several times since with the latest revision in May 2012.

The group was established because there was a need for general liaison on road accident statistics for Scotland, covering both the "STATS19" system and other statistical matters on road accidents. It was to meet periodically to discuss such statistical matters.

The group was set up to discuss any problems with the "STATS19" system. One particular aim was to ensure that the information for Scotland flows smoothly to the main databases - within The Scottish Government, the Transport Scotland Statistics branch's statistical database (covering all injury road accidents) and the Road Network Management and Maintenance Division's SERIS database (covering trunk road accidents); and the GB-wide injury road accidents database held by what is now the Department for Transport (DfT). The group could also consider aspects of police or local authority sources if that were required.

In addition to discussing the "STATS19" system, it was hoped that the group would comment on improving other sources of information on road accidents, and provide feedback on the statistics published by The Scottish Government / Transport Scotland in "Reported Road Casualties Scotland" and its Statistical Bulletins.

Objectives of Group

The main objectives specified for the group are as follows:

  • To improve data quality
  • To liaise on current STATS19 system.
  • To examine any changes proposed by GB on STATS19 system.
  • Consider any problems affecting the smooth updating of existing computer databases.
  • Review use of current published statistics on road accidents in Scotland.
  • Consider topical issues/problems with the data as they arise

Public involvement and engagement

Introduction

Meeting the needs of users is a fundamental part of producing high-quality statistics. The Code of Practice for Statistics states that

“Users of statistics and data should be at the centre of statistical production; their needs should be understood, their views sought and acted on, and their use of statistics supported.”

This Public Involvement and Engagement Strategy sets out how we engage with users of Transport Scotland statistics, how we gather and respond to feedback, and how we ensure that our statistics continue to meet the needs of decision-makers, researchers, stakeholders and the wider public.

Statistical Planning

Publishing information on our statistical plans demonstrates openness and transparency about our priorities and enables users and stakeholders to help shape future work.

Our most recent statistical work programme is available.

We will keep users informed about significant developments in our statistical programme through:

  • The Transport Scotland statistics webpages
  • ScotStat email notifications
  • Stakeholder presentations and engagement events

We welcome comments on our priorities and improvement plans and will consider feedback received throughout the year.

User Feedback

We actively encourage feedback on all aspects of our statistical products and services.

Contact details are provided within each statistical publication and on relevant webpages to enable users to share their views easily.

We are committed to:

  • Reviewing feedback received from users
  • Considering suggestions for improvements
  • Taking account of user needs when developing future outputs
  • Communicating significant changes resulting from user feedback where appropriate

User Groups and Stakeholder Engagement

Regular engagement with user groups helps us understand the needs of different audiences and improve the value of our statistics.

Transport Scotland statisticians participate in and support a range of stakeholder forums, working groups and collaborative networks. Depending on the topic area, these may include:

  • Transport Scotland governance and programme boards
  • Road safety stakeholder groups
  • Scottish Government analytical networks
  • Cross-government statistical groups
  • UK transport statistical networks

These groups provide opportunities for sharing information, discussing user requirements, identifying emerging priorities and improving statistical outputs.

Disability and Transport Statistics Steering Group

The Disability and Transport Statistics publication is supported by a steering group comprising key stakeholders and users.

The steering group provides advice and challenge on:

  • User requirements
  • Data development priorities
  • Opportunities for future analytical work
  • Accessibility and usability of outputs

Road Safety Statistics Engagement

Under the auspices of Scotland's Road Safety Framework, Transport Scotland statisticians regularly present road safety statistics and analysis to a range of stakeholder groups and governance forums.

These engagements provide opportunities for users to:

  • Learn about the latest road safety statistics and trends
  • Ask questions and seek clarification on findings
  • Provide feedback on existing outputs
  • Identify emerging evidence requirements
  • Discuss and commission further analysis from the statistics team where appropriate

User Committees

Transport Scotland has established user committees to provide user input regarding our statistics. These are the Liaison Group for Road Accident Statistics (LGRAS) and the Transport and Travel Statistics Advisory Committee (TTSAC). While these forums are not currently convened on a regular basis, they remain available as recognised channels for user engagement and may be reconvened where this is considered an effective and proportionate means of gathering user views.

Consultations

We will undertake consultation with users where significant changes to statistics, data collections, methodologies or publication arrangements are being considered.

Consultations may be used to:

  • Identify user requirements.
  • Test proposals for change.
  • Assess the impact of changes on data suppliers and users

Consultations will be publicised through Transport Scotland communication channels, ScotStat notifications and relevant stakeholder networks.

We will seek to publish consultation findings and explain how user feedback has informed decisions wherever possible.

Communications, Publications and Events

We use a range of communication channels to keep users informed about statistical developments, forthcoming publications and newly released statistics.

These include:

  • Transport Scotland statistics webpages
  • ScotStat email notifications
  • Transport Scotland social media channels
  • Presentations at user groups and professional networks

In particular, the ScotStat register provides a key mechanism for notifying users about forthcoming and newly published statistical outputs. We use ScotStat notifications to alert subscribers to new publications, consultations, methodological developments and other significant updates relating to Transport Scotland statistics.

Statistics consultations

Current Consultations

The Transport Statistics team do not currently have any formal live consultations. However, we always welcome feedback from users on our publications. Please feel free to contact us with any comments or questions by emailing transtat@transport.gov.scot

Statistical work programme

The work of the Transport Scotland Statistics Team primarily focuses on:

  • Analysis of police-recorded road collisions and casualties.
  • Analysis of transport-related content within the Scottish Household Survey.

Business as Usual Activities

The Transport Scotland Statistics Team will deliver the following key business-as-usual activities during 2026/27:

Statistical Publications

Production of five Official Statistics publications to schedule set out in published in the Scottish Government Official Statistics: forthcoming publications spreadsheet:

  • Key Reported Road Casualties
    Provisional police-recorded road casualty statistics for the previous year, providing headline figures on fatalities and injuries to support road safety monitoring and policy decisions.
  • Reported Road Casualties
    Finalised publication of police-recorded injury road collisions and casualties in Scotland, including detailed analysis by severity, road user type, demographics, location and long-term trends.
  • Transport and Travel in Scotland
    Analysis of Scottish Household Survey transport data, covering topics such as travel behaviour, transport use, accessibility, and satisfaction.
  • Disability and Transport in Scotland
    Examines transport access, travel patterns and inequalities experienced by disabled people in Scotland, supporting equality monitoring and evidence-based policymaking.
  • Scottish Transport Statistics
    Comprehensive transport compendium bringing together data on transport activity, infrastructure, finance, travel patterns, emissions and safety across major transport modes.

Road Safety Framework Monitoring

Provision of analysis to support monitoring and oversight of Scotland's Road Safety Framework. This includes:

  • Production of performance monitoring reports and slide packs.
  • Presentations to governance and stakeholder groups.
  • Producing analysis commissioned by governance and stakeholder groups.

This work supports a range of groups including the Strategic Partnership Board, Operational Partnership Group, Local Partnership Forums and Road User Focus Groups.

Ad Hoc Analysis and Enquiries

Provision of analytical support in response to ad hoc requests, including:

  • Parliamentary Questions.
  • Freedom of Information requests.
  • Other requests from policy officials and stakeholders

Transition to Cloud and Open Source, and Development of Reproducible Analytical Pipelines (RAP)

Significant resources will be invested in updating statistical processes through the adoption of Reproducible Analytical Pipeline (RAP) principles and moving analysis and data sets to open source and cloud solutions, as part of Scottish Government-wide Move 2 programme. This work will include:

  • Migration of analytical processes from SAS to R.
  • Development of open-source analytical workflows.
  • Transition of datasets and processes to cloud-based platforms.
  • Upskilling team members in new analytical approaches.

Whilst this work will not directly result in new statistical outputs, it will improve efficiency, transparency, quality assurance and resilience across the team's analytical processes.

Road Casualty Data Quality and Coverage

The team will undertake further work to assure the quality and coverage of Stats19 road casualty data in Scotland, through comparison with alternative data sources and assessment of the current severity-adjustment methodology.

Statistical Publications and Communication

The team will continue to improve Transport Scotland statistical outputs. Planned activities include:

  • Updating and expanding methodological information accompanying publications based on the Scottish Household Survey.
  • Incorporating findings from the Rail Customer Experience Survey within the Disability and Transport in Scotland publication.
  • Investigating options for updating the Scottish Access to Bus Indicator and assessing the feasibility of future publication.

Quality management approach

Introduction

Transport Scotland is committed to producing high-quality statistics that serve the public good and support informed decision-making. Our statistics are produced in line with the Code of Practice for Statistics and the Scottish Government's Quality Management Policy for Official Statistics.

Working with Data Suppliers, Partners and Users

We work with data providers to understand how data are collected, identify potential quality issues and monitor changes that may affect published statistics. We also engage with external stakeholders, policy colleagues, and other analysts to understand user needs and to investigate any quality concerns that arise.

Users are encouraged to provide feedback on our statistics, and any quality concerns raised are reviewed and addressed where appropriate.

Quality Assurance and Continuous Improvement

Quality assurance is embedded throughout the statistical production process and includes:

  • Automated and manual validation of source data;
  • Checking data against historical trends and related datasets;
  • Reproducibility checks;
  • Review of statistical commentary, tables and charts;
  • Investigation and resolution of anomalies before publication.

Transport Scotland is currently transitioning statistical analysis to open-source analytical software. As we are recreating our statistical analysis in new programming languages, this provides an additional opportunity to quality assure methods, effectively acting as a code audit alongside existing quality assurance procedures.

We monitor the quality of our inputs, data, methods and outputs throughout the statistical lifecycle. This includes assessing completeness, accuracy, consistency, timeliness, methodological suitability, and fitness for purpose. Quality issues are assessed and managed according to their potential impact on users and decision-making.

Quality Management by Data Source

Police-Recorded Road Casualty Statistics (STATS19)

Road casualty statistics are derived from STATS19 records collected by Police Scotland as part of the Great Britain-wide road casualty data collection system.

To support quality management, we:

  • Work closely with Police Scotland data colleagues and road policing colleagues to identify and address quality issues;
  • Monitor the completeness and consistency of STATS19 data through a dedicated quality assurance dashboard, including comparisons with related sources;
  • Undertake enhanced validation of fatal casualty records, reflecting their importance to users and policymakers;
  • Participate in the Department for Transport's Standing Committee on Road Injury Collision Statistics (SCRICS), which provides a forum for statistics producers across Great Britain to investigate quality issues and promote consistent interpretation of STATS19 data.

Identified issues are investigated with data suppliers prior to publication.

Scottish Household Survey Transport Statistics

The Transport and Travel in Scotland and Disability and Transport publications use data from the Scottish Household Survey (SHS), a large-scale Scottish Government survey covering a wide range of topics, including transport.

To support quality management, we work with the Scottish Household Survey team and survey contractors to:

  • Monitor response rates, sample composition and fieldwork outcomes;
  • Review any methodological changes that may affect outputs
  • Review feedback from interviewers regarding operation of transport questions;
  • Undertake quality assurance on provisional datasets.

Scottish Transport Statistics Data Returns

The Scottish Transport Statistics publication draws together data from a wide range of organisations, including transport operators, regulators, local authorities and other public bodies.

To support quality management, we:

  • Maintain regular engagement with data suppliers;
  • Provide opportunities for suppliers to identify changes to data collection systems, methodologies, and definitions;
  • Investigate anomalies and inconsistencies with suppliers.

Communicating Uncertainty and Limitations

We are transparent about the strengths and limitations of our statistics and seek to provide users with sufficient information to judge whether statistics are suitable for their intended use.

Publications and supporting documentation may include information on:

  • data sources and methods;
  • revisions and corrections;
  • comparability over time;
  • data quality strengths and limitations;
  • appropriate interpretation and use of the statistics.

As the SHS in particular is a sample survey, we communicate uncertainty through the provision of confidence intervals in accompanying statistical tables. To support interpretation, in commentary we generally only highlight changes where they are statistically significant.

Where known quality issues remain, these are communicated openly to users.

Balancing the Dimensions of Quality

In producing statistics, we seek to balance the five dimensions of quality identified within the European Statistics Code of Practice: relevance, accuracy, timeliness, accessibility and comparability.

There are occasions where improvements in one dimension may affect another. For example, additional validation may improve accuracy but reduce timeliness, while methodological improvements may affect comparability with historical data.

When making decisions about quality, we consider the intended use of the statistics, user needs, risks associated with quality issues and the benefits and costs of additional quality assurance activity. Our aim is to apply a proportionate approach that delivers statistics that are sufficiently accurate, timely and useful for users.
